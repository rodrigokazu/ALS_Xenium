# Atera cerebellum: a transcript-first segmentation pipeline

Status 2026-10-01: plan, stage 0 (feasibility) running. Code stays in `atera_reseg/`; `paper_reseg_pipeline/` is not edited.

## Why a new structure

The Xenium spinal cord pipeline was built for a 5k gene panel, where transcripts are too sparse to say much about a
single location, so the rules lean on the 18S, DAPI and membrane images. Atera has 60 to 170 transcripts per µm² over
18,050 genes. A 1 to 2 µm neighbourhood holds hundreds of transcripts, which is enough to read the local cell identity
directly. The earlier Atera runs (density growth, identity gate, I2) were patches on the old structure. This plan
rebuilds the order of the steps around that property.

## What the earlier runs taught us

- 18S and DAPI give reliable somata; nucleus seeds miss cells cut without a nucleus (Purkinje profiles).
- Transcript density works as a cytoplasm stain; growth on density alone recovers transcripts but mixes types.
- A simple type gate (I2) cut most of that mixing: +3 points recovery at plain MECR level with 10x.
- The evaluation set-up works and is kept: MECR, MECR at 1,000 tx, held-out windows, block bootstrap, baselines
  at standard settings.

## Stages (each one checked before the next is built)

0. Feasibility. Does local composition tell "same cell" from "different cell" for transcript pairs at matched
   distance? Ground truth: pairs inside one DAPI nucleus vs pairs in two different nuclei. Report separately for
   neighbours of a different type and of the same type (granule next to granule), against density and membrane
   stain features.
1. Transcript features: local composition vector (cluster profiles and an unsupervised gene embedding), soft type
   probability, density.
2. Seeds: nuclei, 18S somata, and compact same-identity blobs without a nucleus.
3. Boundary map: membrane stain, composition change and density valleys combined into one boundary probability.
4. Assignment: grow cells from the seeds over a transcript affinity graph (edge = probability of same cell, learned
   from nucleus pairs); stop where affinity to every seed is low.
5. Split and merge as graph tests: merge when edges across the shared border are as strong as within; split when a
   cell's graph has a weak cut between two compositions. Thresholds at whole-sample scope. Checked on simulated
   errors (two real cells merged, one real cell cut).
6. Neuropil: transcripts with weak affinity to every seed form a separate layer, attached to somata later.
7. Evaluation, fixed before tuning: tuning windows spot1 and spot2; held-out spot3 and held1 to held6; metrics as
   above; 10x and baselines untouched.
